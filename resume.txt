Distance-3 surface-code memory on IQM Emerald
Project summary and rotation-count result

Objective
Store one logical Z bit using nine data qubits and eight ancillas. Reuse the
ancillas after each measurement, execute with zero SWAP gates, and measure
how often the decoded logical bit is wrong after a given number of rounds.

Circuit and decoder
I built a no-reset circuit generator and translated its output from Stim to
Qiskit and then into IQM instructions. I rewrote the detector parities for
reused ancillas. I compiled the stabilizer interactions into CZ gates and
single-qubit rotations, tracked basis changes on each wire, and cancelled
redundant Hadamards. The selected circuit reverses the order of its four CZ
layers every second round. I implemented an approximate likelihood decoder
that considers one- and two-fault explanations and uses matching for
unresolved detection events.

Simulation and its limits
The three supplied papers informed tests of repeated rounds, code distance,
and correlated faults. The broad simulation covered 668 conditions and
4,008,000 shots across three schedules. It included X and Z memories,
distances 3, 5 and 7, and several noise strengths and profiles. In the
distance-3 Z test with balanced noise p = 0.003, the selected circuit and
decoder reduced failures by 17.4% relative to forward ordering with ordinary
matching on fresh validation shots. The simulated 0.243% logical error per
round substantially underestimated the first hardware result. The physical
processor produced more detection events than the assumed noise model.

Hardware investigation
The first Emerald run gave logical failures of 28.70% after 3 rounds and
44.36% after 5. I traced the translation through the IQM instruction list
and verified the ideal measurement parities, signs, bit order and qubit
mapping. The translator dropped Stim's layer boundaries, allowing operations
on different wires to cross those boundaries. I added --preserve-ticks to
translate them into barriers. On the same patch and calibration, the next
run gave 10.05% after 3 rounds and 16.10% after 5 (4,000 shots each).
The two versions had the same gate counts. This comparison suggests that
timing contributed to the original failures. The jobs ran at different times.

Matched comparison with the colleague's project
I ran both circuits in one Emerald job on the colleague's 17-qubit patch,
with their standard dynamical decoupling, the same calibration, no resets
between rounds and zero SWAPs. Each circuit received 4,000 shots at each
length. I aligned the logical Z readout row and scored the raw measurements
using the same BP+OSD decoder and SI1000 noise prior for both circuits.

                        Our circuit             Colleague's circuit
3 rounds                236/4000 =  5.900%       325/4000 =  8.125%
5 rounds                456/4000 = 11.400%       520/4000 = 13.000%
Fitted error per round             2.332%                    2.901%

Using each project's own decoder and prior on those same shots gave fitted
rates of 2.339% for ours and 2.735% for the colleague's. The fits use only
two round counts, so the measured failure counts are the strongest comparison.
I also independently reproduced the colleague's earlier 2.654% five-length
fit from their saved data. They contributed the placement and decoupling
used in the matched test. The circuits differed in rotation count, layer
order and barrier placement, so the job measured their combined effect.

How low can the single-qubit rotation count go?
The resource counted here is one single-qubit rotation instruction in the
submitted IQM circuit. Server-inserted dynamical-decoupling pulses are
outside that count. At 3 rounds our circuit used 94 rotations and 72 CZs.
At 5 rounds it used 150 rotations and 120 CZs. The colleague's circuit
used 104 and 172 rotations respectively, with the same CZ counts.

For the present compiler the optimization is a shortest-path problem.
A state records which wires carry a pending Hadamard basis change. Each
choice of layer order has a cost equal to the number of rotations that
must actually be emitted. If two partial schedules end in the same state,
the cheaper one suffices because their future costs are identical. The
search also charges for the basis needed at final data readout.

Of the 24 permutations of the four fixed interaction layers, four implement
the identical ideal entangling operation for one round. I checked this
using Clifford tableaus and minimized over sequences of those four choices.
The resulting minima were 94 at 3 rounds and 150 at 5. The alternating
schedule attains both and retains graphlike distance 3. At 3 rounds the
94 consist of 48 ancilla rotations and 46 data rotations. At 5 rounds the
split is 80 and 70. Even relaxing the exact-round-operation constraint
and searching all 24 layer orders gives a 3-round lower bound of 84 for
this compiler, already above 60. That relaxed bound allows invalid schedules.

These bounds assume the existing stabilizer interactions, four fixed layers,
CZ synthesis and Hadamard cancellation rules. A proof covering every
possible Emerald circuit would need to include other decompositions and
syndrome-extraction designs. Whether a different valid design can reach
60 rotations at 3 rounds remains open.
